<h2 id="dataset">What did the researchers analyze?</h2>
<p>A team led by Valentina Elce at the IMT School for Advanced Studies Lucca asked 207 adults to describe their dreams and their waking experiences every day for two weeks. The main dataset, collected between 2020 and 2024, holds 3,366 reports. A second set of 351 dream reports came from the spring 2020 lockdown.</p>
<p>Participants also completed tests on sleep, personality and cognition. Three large language models scored each report on 16 dimensions, from emotional intensity to bizarreness. According to the institute, they did about as well as human raters.</p>
<h2 id="patterns">What patterns did they find?</h2>
<p>Compared with waking reports, dreams moved away from thoughts about oneself toward scenes you perceive: places, movement, several characters, odd events. Work, school or hospital settings did show up, but blended into new scenes rather than copied.</p>
<p>Individual traits mattered. People prone to mind-wandering reported more fragmented, fast-changing dreams. Those who valued dreams reported richer, more immersive ones. Sleep quality was linked to dream content too. During lockdown, dreams carried more emotional intensity and more references to limits, effects that faded in the following years.</p>
<p>These are statistical links across a group of volunteers. “Not random” does not mean every image hides a message, and the study cannot tell you what <em>your</em> dream means.</p>

<h2 id="journal">What to note in your dream journal</h2>
<p>The study suggests looking at the shape of a dream, not only its plot. When you wake up, ask: was it mostly images or mostly thoughts? Did the setting keep changing? Could you act, or were you blocked? How strong was the emotion? And how did you sleep?</p>
<p><strong>Fictional example:</strong> “Office that became a train station. I could not find the exit. Anxious, 4/5. Slept badly, deadline Friday.” One entry proves nothing. Ten entries can show whether the blocked exit comes back on short nights or before deadlines.
