                    Dream journal: recall, methods and routines
                </h1>
<p class="text-lg text-purple-200/80 leading-relaxed">
                    The alarm goes off and, for a few seconds, the dream is still there: a corridor, a face, a strange unease. You check your phone, and it’s gone. A <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="dream-journal-guide">dream journal</a> catches that moment. Here are our guides on recall, <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="rem-sleep-dreams">REM sleep</a>, the <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="../guides/dream-symbols-dictionary">meaning of common dream symbols</a> and a morning routine you can keep.
                </p>

<section class="glass-panel rounded-2xl p-6 md:p-8 mt-10 border border-dream-salmon/10" data-blog-hub-expanded="true">
  <h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">How to start a dream journal</h2>
  <div class="prose prose-invert prose-purple max-w-none text-gray-300 leading-relaxed space-y-4">
    <p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> note your dream before you get up, even a single image, every morning for a week. Regularity matters more than length.</p>
    <ul><li><strong>On waking:</strong> stay still and replay the dream.</li><li><strong>What to note:</strong> place, people, action, feeling.</li><li><strong>After seven nights:</strong> reread and spot what comes back.</li></ul>
    <p>The <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="/en/voice-dream-journal">voice dream journal on Android</a> lets you speak a dream as soon as you wake: it is transcribed and saved, ready to reread alongside the others.</p>
  </div>
</section>
