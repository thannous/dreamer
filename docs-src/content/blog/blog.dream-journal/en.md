<h1 class="font-serif text-3xl md:text-5xl mb-6 text-transparent bg-clip-text bg-gradient-to-b from-white via-dream-lavender to-purple-400/50 leading-tight">
                    Dream journal templates: 5 formats to copy
                </h1>
<p class="text-lg text-purple-200/80 leading-relaxed">
                    You open the notebook, pen in hand, and the blank page chases off whatever was left of the dream. A template fixes that: a few fields, always in the same order, that you fill in without thinking. Here are five dream journal templates, each built for one specific need, with a filled-in fictional example. For the habit itself (when to write, how to keep going), read our <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="dream-journal-guide">guide to keeping a dream journal</a>.
                </p>
</header>

<section class="glass-panel rounded-2xl p-6 mb-8 border border-dream-salmon/20 bg-white/5" aria-labelledby="quick-answer-title" data-blog-hub-expanded="true">
  <h2 id="quick-answer-title" class="font-serif text-xl text-dream-cream mb-3">Which template for which need?</h2>
  <ul class="space-y-2 text-purple-100/80 leading-relaxed">
    <li><strong>Only a fragment is left:</strong> <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="#quick-template">the quick template</a>, about thirty seconds.</li>
    <li><strong>The dream is still vivid:</strong> <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="#full-template">the full morning template</a>.</li>
    <li><strong>The same nightmare keeps coming back:</strong> <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="#recurring-template">the recurring dream template</a>.</li>
    <li><strong>You are working on lucid dreaming:</strong> <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="#lucid-template">the dream sign template</a>.</li>
    <li><strong>You want to link a dream to your day:</strong> <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="#day-template">the dream and day template</a>.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<div class="prose max-w-none text-gray-300">

<section id="quick-template" class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">1. The quick template: what to write when only a fragment is left</h2>
<p>This is the template for rushed mornings and dreams that are already fraying. It accepts incomplete entries: a single image is enough. The goal is not to tell the story but to keep a handhold, so other details can surface while you write.</p>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-white/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-dream-salmon uppercase mb-3">Template to copy</p>
<ol class="space-y-1 text-sm text-gray-200 font-mono">
<li>Date:</li>
<li>Place:</li>
<li>Feeling (one word):</li>
<li>Image that stayed:</li>
<li>Working title:</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-dream-salmon/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-purple-300/70 uppercase mb-3">Fictional example</p>
<p class="text-sm">Date: Tuesday. Place: a train station, but carpeted. Feeling: rushed. Image: a ticket with the ink running. Title: “The train with no timetable”.</p>
</div>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> write the title last. Looking for it is often what brings a second detail back.</p>
</section>

<section id="full-template" class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">2. The full morning template: how to record a dream that is still vivid</h2>
<p>When the dream is all there, with its people and its twists, this template sorts it into an order that pays off when you reread. The fields stay the same from one morning to the next, which is what lets you compare entries a month from now.</p>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-white/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-dream-salmon uppercase mb-3">Template to copy</p>
<ol class="space-y-1 text-sm text-gray-200 font-mono">
<li>Date and wake-up time:</li>
<li>Setting (place, era, light):</li>
<li>People there:</li>
<li>What happens, in order:</li>
<li>Feeling during the dream:</li>
<li>Feeling on waking:</li>
<li>Odd or impossible detail:</li>
<li>One line about yesterday (optional):</li>
<li>Title:</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-dream-salmon/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-purple-300/70 uppercase mb-3">Fictional example</p>
<p class="text-sm">Woke at 6:40. Setting: my grandparents’ flat, but far too big, with a hallway that never ends. People: my sister, a neighbour I don’t know. What happens: we look for a room with a piano in it; every door opens onto a kitchen. Feeling during: curious, then annoyed. On waking: a soft nostalgia. Odd detail: every clock says noon. Yesterday: my sister called about selling the flat. Title: “The row of kitchens”.</p>
</div>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> keep the feeling in the dream separate from the feeling on waking. They often differ, and the gap is usually where there is something to think about.</p>
</section>

<section id="recurring-template" class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">3. The recurring dream template: what to track when the same nightmare returns</h2>
<p>A recurring dream feels identical night after night. Written down with the same fields, it often shows what does change: a different place, a way out that appears, fear that eases. This template is for spotting those differences and following intensity over time.</p>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-white/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-dream-salmon uppercase mb-3">Template to copy</p>
<ol class="space-y-1 text-sm text-gray-200 font-mono">
<li>Date:</li>
<li>The scene that repeats (one sentence):</li>
<li>What was different this time:</li>
<li>Fear, from 0 to 10:</li>
<li>Did it wake me up? yes / no</li>
<li>Context from the day before (stress, schedule, alcohol, late screens):</li>
<li>What I wish I had done in the dream:</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-dream-salmon/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-purple-300/70 uppercase mb-3">Fictional example</p>
<p class="text-sm">Scene: I’m late for an exam and can’t find the room. Different this time: someone pointed the way. Fear: 5 out of 10, down from 8 last week. Woke me up: no. Day before: performance review postponed. What I wish I had done: ask someone for the time.</p>
</div>
<p>Reread these entries once a week rather than every morning: the trend matters more than a single night. The last field is there on purpose. Rewriting the ending of a nightmare while awake, then rehearsing the new version in your mind, is the core of imagery rehearsal therapy, which the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends for nightmare disorder (<a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.7178" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Morgenthaler et al., 2018</a>).</p>
<p><strong>When to talk to a doctor:</strong> if nightmares come several times a week, keep you from falling back asleep or make you dread going to bed, a doctor or sleep specialist can help. A journal does not replace that appointment, but it makes it more precise.</p>
</section>

<section id="lucid-template" class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">4. The dream sign template: what to log if you are practising lucid dreaming</h2>
<p>To become lucid, you first have to notice you are dreaming. The oddities that come back from one dream to the next (a light switch that won’t work, a childhood place, being able to float) are your dream signs. This template pulls them out so you can count them.</p>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-white/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-dream-salmon uppercase mb-3">Template to copy</p>
<ol class="space-y-1 text-sm text-gray-200 font-mono">
<li>Date:</li>
<li>Dream summary (three lines):</li>
<li>Dream sign spotted afterwards:</li>
<li>Did I know I was dreaming? no / briefly / yes</li>
<li>If yes: what tipped me off, what I did, what ended the lucidity:</li>
<li>Sign to watch for tomorrow:</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-dream-salmon/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-purple-300/70 uppercase mb-3">Fictional example</p>
<p class="text-sm">Summary: packing for a trip, the suitcase fills itself, I miss the bus. Sign spotted: the text on my phone changed every time I looked, third time this month. Lucid: briefly, then the dream carried on. Sign to watch: read any text twice.</p>
</div>
<p>After two or three weeks, count the signs. The one that comes back most becomes your reality check during the day. Our <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="lucid-dreaming">lucid dreaming guide</a> covers the techniques.</p>
</section>

<section id="day-template" class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">5. The dream and day template: how to link a dream to your day without forcing it</h2>
<p>Researchers find that dreams often pick up concerns and activities from waking life, more for some kinds of activity than others (<a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/S1053-8100(02)00072-7" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Schredl and Hofmann, 2003</a>). This template helps you spot those echoes without tying everything back to yesterday: many dreams have no obvious link, and that is normal.</p>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-white/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-dream-salmon uppercase mb-3">Template to copy</p>
<ol class="space-y-1 text-sm text-gray-200 font-mono">
<li>The dream in three lines:</li>
<li>Three moments from yesterday (facts, not analysis):</li>
<li>A feeling shared by the dream and the day, if any:</li>
<li>What the dream changed or exaggerated:</li>
<li>One open question, no answer required:</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5 my-6 border border-dream-salmon/10">
<p class="text-xs font-mono text-purple-300/70 uppercase mb-3">Fictional example</p>
<p class="text-sm">Dream: I’m painting a room and the colour changes whenever I step back. Yesterday: a renovation quote arrived, dinner with a friend, a long meeting. Shared feeling: not keeping up with deadlines. Exaggeration: paint that changes on its own. Question: “What do I feel I have no grip on right now?”</p>
</div>
<p><strong>Tip:</strong> list yesterday’s facts before rereading the dream. Otherwise you pick the ones that fit and build a link that isn’t there.</p>
</section>

<section class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-4">Notebook, note or app: where to keep these templates</h2>
<p>A notebook on the nightstand, with the template copied onto the first page, is still the simplest. A phone note works too, as long as you switch to airplane mode so you don’t land on your messages. If writing first thing is hard, speak instead: Noctalia’s <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="/en/voice-dream-journal">voice dream journal</a> transcribes what you say and keeps each dream next to the others for rereading. To compare the apps out there, see our <a class="text-dream-salmon hover:underline" href="/en/dream-journal-apps">comparison of dream journal apps</a>.</p>
<p>Whatever you use, keep the same template for at least a month. Changing fields every week makes rereading impossible.</p>
</section>

<section class="mt-12">
<h2 class="font-serif text-2xl text-dream-cream mb-6">Frequently asked questions</h2>
<div class="space-y-4">
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5">
<h3 class="font-serif text-lg text-dream-cream mb-2">Which dream journal template should I start with?</h3>
<p class="text-sm text-gray-300">The quick template: place, feeling, one image and a title. It takes about thirty seconds and still works when only a fragment is left. Move to the full template once writing in the morning has become a habit.</p>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5">
<h3 class="font-serif text-lg text-dream-cream mb-2">Can I use more than one template in the same journal?</h3>
<p class="text-sm text-gray-300">Yes. Keep the quick or full template for every morning and add a specialised one only when it helps: a recurring nightmare, a lucid dream, a heavy day. What matters is filling in the same fields each time so entries can be compared.</p>
</div>
<div class="glass-panel rounded-xl p-5">
<h3 class="font-serif text-lg text-dream-cream mb-2">Should I interpret a dream while I write it down?</h3>
<p class="text-sm text-gray-300">No. Write what you saw and felt first, and keep interpretation for when you reread. A meaning added too early tends to replace the memory itself.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>
