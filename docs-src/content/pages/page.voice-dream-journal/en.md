    <h2 class="text-2xl font-serif text-white mb-4">What to say in the first 60 seconds after waking</h2>
    <p>You don't need the whole story. Stay in bed, start recording and talk without polishing. If you're not sure where to begin, use this order.</p>
    <ol class="list-decimal pl-5 mt-4 space-y-2">
      <li><strong class="text-white">The clearest image.</strong> Start with whatever is still sharp: a face, a room, an object.</li>
      <li><strong class="text-white">Place and people.</strong> Say who was there, even if it's vague: “a coworker, I think”.</li>
      <li><strong class="text-white">What was happening.</strong> Tell the action in the order it comes back to you. Sorting out the dream's real order can wait for the review.</li>
      <li><strong class="text-white">How you felt.</strong> Name the feeling inside the dream, then the one you woke up with if it's different.</li>
      <li><strong class="text-white">The detail that doesn't fit.</strong> A clock with no hands, rain indoors, a word someone said. Say it even if it seems minor.</li>
    </ol>
    <p class="mt-4">Ums and half sentences are fine. Before you save, Noctalia shows you the transcribed text and you can fix it with the keyboard. You can also edit it later from your journal.</p>
