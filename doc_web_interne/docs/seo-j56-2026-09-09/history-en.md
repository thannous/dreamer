## Textes EN proposés (brouillon agent, pas contenu déjà approuvé/publié)

### Introduction / réponse courte

> People have interpreted dreams for millennia. Surviving texts from Mesopotamia and Egypt offer examples of dreams understood as omens or divine encounters. Later writers also explored natural and psychological explanations. This history traces several of those approaches while separating historical beliefs from findings in sleep research.

### Exemple mésopotamien / case chronologie

> Around 2120–2110 BCE: Gudea's temple narrative. A clay cylinder now held by the Louvre describes a dream in which the god Ningirsu gives Gudea instructions for a temple. It is a documented example of the religious role of dreams in ancient Mesopotamia, rather than proof of when dream interpretation first began.

### Égypte / case chronologie

> Around 1220 BCE: an Egyptian dream manual. The British Museum dates Papyrus Chester Beatty III to this period. Its dream book records an approach to interpreting dreams as omens. Such a manuscript documents a historical practice; it does not validate its predictions.

### Remplacement du paragraphe 65 %

> Dreams can draw on waking experiences. In a 2003 diary study, 29 participants recorded dreams and daily experiences over 14 days. Researchers reported that 65% of 299 sleep reports included aspects of recent waking life, while only 1–2% replayed an episode. This was a finding about reports in one study, not a universal percentage of dream content.

### Freud / FAQ correspondante

> Freud did not invent dream interpretation. In The Interpretation of Dreams, he developed a psychoanalytic account of dreams in terms of wishes, associations and disguised meanings. This was an influential historical theory, rather than the first attempt to explain dreams psychologically.

### Recherche moderne

> In 1953, Aserinsky and Kleitman reported recurring periods of rapid eye movements during sleep and investigated their association with dream recall. Later research has examined dreaming in both REM and non-REM sleep. Physiological measurements help researchers study dream experience, but they do not by themselves reveal what a particular dream means.

> Researchers have proposed several accounts of dreaming, including Revonsuo's threat-simulation hypothesis. A proposed function is different from an established explanation for every dream. Historical interpretation, personal reflection and experimental sleep research address related but distinct questions.

### Cultures / FAQ correspondante

> Dream beliefs should be described in their specific historical and cultural setting. A single account cannot represent every member of a religion, region or community. When reading an interpretation, ask who recorded it, when, and whether it describes a belief, a personal practice or a finding from research.

### Conclusion

> The history of dream interpretation brings together different ways of asking what dreams mean. Ancient texts document beliefs and practices; psychological theories offer interpretive frameworks; sleep experiments investigate measurable aspects of dreaming. Keeping those forms of evidence distinct makes the history more useful without turning any one approach into a universal code.
